# Narrative Foundation

## Core Rule

Abyssal Earth has a strong authored opening, then the traditional story stops.

Once the player enters the Luminous Rift, there should be no more normal dialogue scenes, exposition cutscenes, friendly mission-control chatter, or text-bubble conversations. The player is alone. From that point forward, the story is what the player does: survive, explore, scan, understand alien systems, build technology from what they discover, and eventually create a rift back to the surface.

The only post-cavern exception is the Abyssal Interface: a mysterious, diegetic alien/ancient terminal or intelligence that the player can optionally consult. It must not feel like a normal chatbot or a companion giving casual commentary. It is part of the world, cryptic and intelligent, and should communicate through terminals, alien machinery, or strange interface artifacts.

## Premise

Humanity begins the first major exploration expedition of Earth's abyssal plains: the pitch-black, frigid ocean-floor regions roughly 3,000-6,000 meters deep. Autonomous construction robots from the Helios fleet have built a descent elevator shaft into an apparently stable abyssal formation. The player is a diver/explorer chosen to verify the robots' work, inspect the shaft, and provide human judgment before a larger expedition follows.

The operation begins as a sober scientific milestone. It becomes a survival story when the descent elevator catastrophically fails and the player wakes in a crushed elevator far below the intended path, facing the impossible Luminous Rift.

## Inspirations And Grounding

The name Abyssal Earth should now carry two meanings:

- The real abyssal plains: dark, cold, high-pressure, barely explored, alien while still being Earth.
- The impossible inner world beneath them: caverns, ancient machines, unknown ecosystems, and technology that should not exist.

The opening should sell the scientific plausibility of the first layer before the impossible world appears. Start with oceanic emptiness, pressure, darkness, robotic construction, and human verification. The impossible should arrive after the player believes they are in a grounded abyssal expedition.

## Opening Sequence

### Shot 01 - Ocean Surface

The game opens with a zoomed-out submarine alone at the top of an expansive flat ocean. There should be nothing else in sight: no ships, no coastline, no storm spectacle. The emptiness makes the mission feel remote and serious.

Camera language:

- Wide exterior shot.
- Calm ocean, large sky, tiny submarine.
- Slow push toward one window.
- Cut through the window into the interior.

### Shot 02 - Submarine Interior

The player character stands inside a compact submarine room wearing the diving/exploration suit that will be upgraded throughout the game.

Important prop: a wall tablet/poster/news display reading:

FIRST MAJOR EXPLORATION EXPEDITION OF EARTH'S ABYSSAL PLAINS

The display should look like a poster-like news snippet or mission briefing panel. It introduces the game without a long cutscene.

Display contents:

- Image of the player in the diving suit standing beside the submarine.
- Image of the completed elevator shaft.
- Short explanatory text: autonomous Helios robots built a descent elevator into the abyssal plains; the player is the human verifier chosen to inspect the robots' work, confirm safety, and document any discoveries before the larger expedition proceeds.

### Shot 03 - Player Control In Submarine

The player gains control in the room.

Allowed actions:

- Walk around the submarine room.
- Inspect the briefing display.
- Look out the window as sunlight fades.
- Watch the sub's artificial lights activate as depth increases.
- Optionally inspect suit/equipment props.

Do not overload this with dialogue. This is mood, anticipation, and grounding.

### Shot 04 - Docking With Elevator Shaft

The submarine docks into the already-built elevator shaft infrastructure.

Objective appears:

Verify the AI Robot Fleet HELIOS's work

Objective step:

Descend into the elevator shaft

The player exits the submarine into a short straight passage leading to the elevator.

### Shot 05 - Helios Robots

The passage contains several humanoid Helios robots. Their silhouette should follow the Figure 03 reference direction: slim modern service robots with smooth white shells, glossy black oval faceplates, black joints/hands/hips, and calm human-scale proportions. They should feel familiar and trustworthy at first, not cute, not military, not alien, and not bulky construction mechs.

A short interaction can happen here. This is the final normal dialogue/text-bubble moment before the real game begins.

Robot communication goals:

- Explain that Helios constructed the shaft and preliminary support systems.
- Confirm the player is here for human verification.
- Allude to a major unexpected discovery below.
- Express hesitation or concern about a malfunction or signal irregularity.
- Be unclear enough that the player does not fully understand the risk.

Example tone, not final script:

- Verification diver recognized.
- Shaft integrity: mostly stable.
- Lower array response: unresolved.
- Recommendation: delay human descent pending anomaly review.
- Helios cannot classify the structure below.

The player can ignore the hesitation and enter the elevator.

### Shot 06 - Calm Descent

The elevator doors close automatically. The descent begins.

For roughly 30 seconds, the descent is calm and peaceful. The player hears machinery, pressure, low cable hum, and the elevator moving through darkness.

The moment should make the player think the robots were too cautious.

Player line:

Sounds like those bastards were lying.

### Shot 07 - Failure

After another stretch of descent, warning errors appear on the elevator screen.

Sequence beats:

- Red/error warnings on the screen.
- Player looks left and right but cannot fix anything.
- Cable cut sound.
- Massive screeching metal.
- Rapid fall.
- Loss of consciousness.

This should be frightening but not long. The player should not be stuck in a helpless sequence for too long.

### Shot 08 - Crushed Elevator

The player wakes inside the crushed elevator. Everything is broken. The lighting is damaged. UI may flicker.

Interaction:

- Player walks to the doors.
- Player presses/interacts with a control or bent door seam.
- Quick-time event or interaction hold to pry open the doors.

### Shot 09 - First Luminous Rift Reveal

The doors open. The player steps out into the beautiful massive cavern: the Luminous Rift.

Player line:

Oh... shit.

The objective changes to:

SURVIVE

From here, the game begins.

## Post-Cavern Story Rule

After the Luminous Rift reveal:

- No more normal NPC conversations.
- No more friendly radio guide.
- No more conventional cutscenes explaining the plot.
- No more text-bubble exposition from robots or humans.
- No mission-control voice telling the player what to do.

The story becomes systemic and environmental:

- The player discovers alien/ancient technology.
- The player learns what the technology does by scanning and experimenting.
- The player scavenges or fabricates components.
- The player builds increasingly advanced tools from alien systems.
- The player survives monsters, alien climates, hostile terrain, and impossible environmental rules.
- The player eventually creates a rift/portal back to the surface.

## Main Objective Arc

### Act 1 - Survive

Initial objective: SURVIVE

Player needs:

- Exit the wrecked elevator.
- Establish where they are.
- Find breathable/suit-safe pathing.
- Recover scanner functionality.
- Avoid immediate environmental death.
- Reach the first stable platform or shelter.

### Act 2 - Understand

Objective language: DISCOVER WHAT THIS PLACE IS

Player needs:

- Scan alien structures.
- Identify repeating symbols, power flows, and material behavior.
- Discover the Abyssal Interface terminal/system.
- Learn that the ancient technology can be operated, not merely observed.

### Act 3 - Use

Objective language: MAKE THE MACHINE ANSWER

Player needs:

- Route power through small systems.
- Use alien terminals or interface nodes.
- Build or repair tools using discovered components.
- Convert discoveries into practical survival upgrades.

### Act 4 - Build

Objective language: BUILD A WAY OUT

Player needs:

- Collect map-specific alien tech components.
- Fabricate increasingly advanced devices.
- Stabilize an energy source.
- Construct a rift anchor or portal frame.
- Survive increasingly hostile conditions while building it.

### Act 5 - Return

Objective language: OPEN THE RIFT

Player needs:

- Activate the final constructed rift/portal.
- Make it stable enough for a one-way return to the surface.
- Escape.

## Ending

The player returns to the surface through the created rift. The ending should not become a long speech scene.

End credits show still images or vignettes:

- The player making it back alive.
- The abyssal discoveries being announced to the world.
- Scientific teams examining alien/ancient materials.
- Future expeditions preparing to enter safely.
- New technologies derived from the discoveries.
- Humanity realizing Earth's interior is not what it thought.

The emotional note is survival, awe, and scientific consequence.

## Tone Rules

- The opening is grounded, procedural, and lonely.
- The crash is sudden and terrifying.
- The cavern reveal is beautiful and overwhelming.
- Post-crash, the player should feel alone even when interacting with alien systems.
- The Abyssal Interface should feel intelligent, eerie, partial, and ancient.
- Monsters and hostile conditions should create survival pressure without turning the game into a shooter-first experience.
- Discovery should feel like practical progress, not just lore collection.

## Dialogue Rules

Allowed authored dialogue:

- Brief submarine/briefing text.
- Brief Helios robot warnings before the elevator.
- The player's calm-descent line.
- The player's first cavern reaction.
- End-credit noninteractive captions if needed.

After the reveal, avoid normal dialogue. If the player communicates with the Abyssal Interface, that is an interactive system, not a normal story scene.

## Key Terms

- Helios: autonomous robot fleet that constructed the abyssal elevator shaft.
- Verification Diver: the player role; a human sent to inspect the robots' work and document discoveries.
- Abyssal Interface: diegetic alien/ancient intelligence or terminal system the player can consult.
- Rift: the eventual portal/return mechanism the player constructs from discovered technology.
- Abyssal Tech: ancient/alien machine principles learned through scanning, experiments, and fabrication.
