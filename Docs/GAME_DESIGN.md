# Abyssal Earth - Game Design

## High Concept

The player explores massive cavern systems deep inside Earth that should not exist: luminous fungal forests, crystal oceans, floating mineral shelves, ancient geothermal gardens, and alien-looking ecosystems sealed away from the surface.

The game is about awe, curiosity, and steady descent. The player is not conquering the underworld; they are documenting it, surviving it, and slowly understanding why Earth contains these impossible spaces.

## Pillars

1. Beauty first: every major space should feel like a screenshot worth keeping.
2. Exploration over combat: tension comes from scale, navigation, unknown biology, weather, and environmental hazards.
3. Discovery has weight: scanning, naming, sketching, mapping, and revisiting discoveries should matter.
4. Human smallness: the player should feel competent but tiny against the cavern worlds.
5. Natural mystery: alien landscapes should still feel geologic, ancient, and physically grounded.

## Core Loop

1. Enter a new cavern pocket.
2. Survey from a dramatic overlook.
3. Pick routes using light, sound, landmarks, and scanner hints.
4. Traverse hazards and terrain.
5. Discover organisms, minerals, ruins, or anomalies.
6. Scan and log findings to unlock route knowledge or equipment upgrades.
7. Reach a deeper gate, elevator shaft, borehole, river, or tectonic passage.

## Player Fantasy

The player is a field explorer sent below Earth's crust after impossible sensor readings. They carry a scanner, climbing tools, deployable beacons, and a field journal. Their strength is observation, persistence, and careful route-finding.

## Initial Mechanics

- First-person movement with sprint, crouch, and mantle/climb hooks.
- Scanner pulse that highlights nearby discoveries and route hints.
- Discovery log with entries for biomes, minerals, lifeforms, and structures.
- Deployable navigation beacons with colored light.
- Environmental hazards: heat, toxic spores, unstable crystal growth, flooding, darkness.
- Photo mode or observation mode as an early priority because visuals are central.

## First Biome: The Luminous Rift

The Luminous Rift is now anchored to the core concept reference at `Content/ArtDirection/References/luminous_rift_core_reference.png`.

A vast vertical cavern opens around an ancient machine complex buried inside Earth. Black basalt, blue crystals, suspended platforms, and monumental carved structures surround a radiant blue-white energy sphere. Warm gold beams connect the sphere to hexagonal collector panels, implying that the cavern is not only geologic but also part of an enormous dormant mechanism.

Primary objective: cross from the descent elevator to the far-side Second Sky overlook by navigating six landmarks:

- First Overlook: the initial concept-art reveal from a dark foreground ledge.
- Abyssal Approach: broken rock ledges and ancient bridge spans descending into the rift.
- Crystal Galleries: blue crystals growing through carved wall panels.
- Collector Array: central orb, gold beams, radial hub, and hex collector clusters.
- Ancient Gate: a monumental wall with circular blue-lit mechanisms.
- Second Sky Overlook: final view into an even deeper lower cavern.

Final beat: scan the Second Sky vista and realize the visible machine is only one layer of a much larger underworld.

Earlier marsh, fungus, and geothermal ideas are preserved as future biome candidates, but they should not define the first map's visual identity.

## Tone

Quiet wonder with moments of danger. More `Subnautica`, `Journey`, `Outer Wilds`, and `Avatar` bioluminescence than shooter or horror. Darkness should frame beauty rather than swallow it.
