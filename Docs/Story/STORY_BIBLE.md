# AbyssalEarth — Story Bible

*The complete narrative reference. This document is world truth — everything here is canon; what the player learns is a strict subset.*

---

## Logline

A deep-sea engineer sent to inspect a robot-built elevator shaft survives a catastrophic fall into an impossible cavern beneath the abyssal plain — a transit hub built by something older than geology — and must learn to make the machine answer before they can build their way home.

---

## The World Truth

### What the cavern is
The cavern system — the player names it nothing; the terminals call it **The Confluence** — is a transit node. Not a city, not a temple: infrastructure. A train station the size of a country, built by entities the terminals call **the third stratum**, operating on a timescale where continental drift is scheduling.

The Confluence predates multicellular life. It was not built *under* the Earth; the Earth accreted *around* it. The fossils in Fossil Sky are not native — they are passengers. Things that came through, died here, and were absorbed into the rock over hundreds of millions of years.

### The third stratum
Never shown. Never named beyond the terminal designation. The game's discipline: **the builders do not appear**. Every environmental detail implies them — the scale of the doors, the gravity engineering, the amber warmth of the Rift Chamber — but the player never meets one. The Confluence is unmaintained but not abandoned; the distinction matters. An abandoned station decays. An unmaintained one waits.

### Why gravity is broken
The Confluence's transit mechanism works by locally redefining "down." The Gravity Well is not damaged — it is *parked midway through a reconfiguration* that was never completed. The gravity fractures, the floating fossil platforms, the reorientation fields: all of it is machinery frozen mid-operation, like an escalator stopped between floors.

### The anomaly HELIOS detected
HELIOS's deep sensors detected the Confluence's *idle power signature* — a rhythmic pulse the robots correctly classified as non-geological. They reported it as an "unresolved anomaly" and recommended delay. The expedition sponsors overrode them. The elevator was built directly above a Confluence ventilation shaft; the "structural anomaly in shaft sector 7" that cut the cable was the Confluence's pressure equalisation cycle — a breath.

The crash was not an attack. **The station did not notice the player arrive.** This is the horror and the comfort of the entire game: the player is too small to be threatened.

### The biological contaminant
When Terminal Gamma registers the player as "Observer entity — access tier: Limited," it is executing a hospitality protocol written for lost passengers. The Confluence has seen stranded travellers before. The Rift protocol the terminal unlocks is the standard service for them: a door home. The player never realises they are using customer service.

## Character

### The player character ("the Engineer")
Unnamed, voiced sparingly. Mid-career structural engineer, hired to verify robot construction work — a clipboard job. Competent, dry-humoured, allergic to wonder as a defence mechanism ("I need to stop saying 'impossible'"). Their arc: from verifying someone else's work to building their own. The final fabricated object of the game — the Rift Stabiliser — is the first thing in the story they design rather than inspect.

**Voice rules**: short lines, no exposition, never narrates what the player can see. Speaks more in the first hour than the last five — the deeper they go, the quieter they get. The silence is characterisation.

### HELIOS
The autonomous construction fleet. Polite, precise, incapable of lying but capable of being ignored. HELIOS units are the only "characters" who told the truth from the start — the prologue warning is the game's entire tragedy in four lines. The bent HELIOS chassis fragment in the wrecked elevator and the dead maintenance drone in Deep Channel are deliberate echoes: the machines came down here too, and didn't do as well as the player.

### The Confluence (as character)
The antagonist that isn't. It never acts against the player. Its hazards are HVAC. Its creatures are pigeons in the rafters. Its terminals are a help desk running at 14% index integrity. The game's tension comes entirely from scale-mismatch, never from malice.

## Act Structure

| Act | Objective | Maps | Emotional beat |
|-----|-----------|------|----------------|
| Prologue | VERIFY HELIOS'S WORK | Submarine → Access Passage → Descent Elevator → Wrecked Elevator | Professional routine → catastrophe |
| 1 | SURVIVE | Luminous Rift → Deep Channel | Awe and terror → first competence |
| 2 | DISCOVER WHAT THIS PLACE IS | Fossil Sky Upper → Fossil Sky | Curiosity → understanding the scale |
| 3 | MAKE THE MACHINE ANSWER | Gravity Well → Gravity Well Lower | Frustration → dialogue with the ancient |
| 4 | BUILD A WAY OUT | Mantle Garden → Mantle Garden Deep | Mastery — the engineer engineers |
| 5 | OPEN THE RIFT | Rift Chamber | Resolution — and the choice to return |

### Act-by-act narrative spine

**Prologue.** Establish the mundane: a funded expedition, a checklist, a window with nothing but black. The HELIOS warning lands as bureaucratic noise. The descent takes 30 seconds to destroy everything. Rule: nothing supernatural is shown until PRO_009 — the player must believe they're in a hard-science story right up until the cavern reveal.

**Act 1.** The player is prey. Vitals matter, hazards kill, the Depth Lurker owns Z4. Discovery is survival information first, wonder second. The act ends when the player crosses Deep Channel — their first deliberate, planned traversal. Competence is earned, not given.

**Act 2.** The scan mechanic becomes the story. Fossil Sky's passengers-in-the-rock reframe everything: this place has *history*, which means it has *function*. The act's final discovery (the carved patterns matching the Luminous Rift monoliths) is the player's own deduction — the game never states it.

**Act 3.** The terminals speak. Three beats of cosmology, delivered in the driest possible voice (a help desk at 14% integrity), and one beat of devastating comedy: the anomaly log classifying the player as a negligible biological contaminant. The player's response — "That's me. I'm the biological contaminant." — is the emotional centre of the game: the moment they accept their scale and stop fighting it.

**Act 4.** The quiet act. Almost no dialogue. The player has a recipe list and a hostile environment, and the game trusts the loop. The single line at the tier-3 forge — "Okay. I know what this needs to be." — is the arc completing: the inspector has become the builder.

**Act 5.** The Rift Chamber asks for everything the player has learned: every hazard type, every traversal skill, the power-routing literacy from Act 3, the stabiliser from Act 4. Then it opens the door. The final line before the fade — "Whatever this place is — whatever you are — I'll be back. I have to be." — commits the character to the sequel hook without undermining the ending.

## Themes

1. **Scale without malice.** The universe is not hostile; it is indifferent at a magnitude that feels like hostility until you stop taking it personally.
2. **Verification vs. creation.** The Engineer's job was to check other people's work. The abyss makes them an author.
3. **The machines were right.** HELIOS told the truth and was overridden. Every dead robot the player finds is a quiet repetition of the theme: being correct is not the same as being heeded.

## Canon rules (for all future content)
- The third stratum never appears, speaks, or acts on-screen.
- The Confluence never targets the player. All danger is environmental indifference.
- The Engineer never gets a name, face reveal, or backstory beyond the job.
- Terminals are the only source of cosmology, and they are unreliable (14% index integrity) — every "fact" they state may be wrong.
- HELIOS units never express emotion, but their phrasing may imply it.
